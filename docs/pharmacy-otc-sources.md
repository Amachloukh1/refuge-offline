# Household pharmacy: German OTC examples

Reviewed against the primary sources below on **2026-11-01**. The same bounded claims are translated into French, German, English and Arabic in `src/household-pharmacy-text.js`; Russian, Chinese, Spanish and Portuguese retain their labelled English fallback.

This is an illustrative selection by symptom, not a personal prescription, diagnosis, exhaustive stockpile or instruction to buy everything. No dosage or purchase links are supplied. Ingredient names do not determine German prescription status on their own: strength, presentation, indication and pack size can change it. Children, pregnancy, chronic conditions and concurrent medicines require individual pharmacy advice and the actual product leaflet.

## Cross-cutting source checks

- [Federal health portal: Hausapotheke](https://gesund.bund.de/hausapotheke): select supplies for the household, seek pharmacy advice, keep original packaging/leaflets, store dry and away from heat and children, check expiry and replace used supplies. Prescription medicines remain a matter for the treating clinician; leftover prescriptions are not a self-medication menu.
- [AMVV Annex 1](https://www.gesetze-im-internet.de/amvv/anlage_1.html): exceptions for paracetamol, ibuprofen and loperamide are conditional. The interface intentionally does not turn these legal thresholds into dosing instructions.
- No antibiotics, systemic steroids, prescription-only treatment, shopping cart, Amazon drug recommendation or automatic medicine order is added.

## Rendering contract

Rows retain `[id, title, text, kind]`. The seven medicine IDs in order are `pain`, `saline`, `electrolytes`, `diarrhea`, `allergy`, `heartburn`, `antiseptic`; three equipment IDs follow with `kind: supply`. The `saline` ID is retained for compatibility while its title now covers a blocked nose and differentiates its examples. Illustrations are category aids, not product packaging, treatment instructions or evidence of a drug's suitability.
